User Guide

How to use the map, the tracking spreadsheet, and the Route Planner.

🗺 Using the map 📊 The spreadsheet 📋 Route Planner

Using the map

Every road is coloured by its current status:

Complete — done
In Progress — partway through
Planned — next up
Not Started — not touched yet

Finding your way around

  • Status filters — tap a status in the sidebar to hide/show it on the map.
  • Road search — type a road name and tap a result to jump to it.
  • Ward list — search or tick individual wards, or "Select / deselect all".
  • ⊕ Find my ward — uses your phone's location to jump straight to the ward you're standing in and filter the map to it.
  • ◉ Live tracking — keeps the map centred on you as you walk your round.
  • On mobile, tap ☰ to open/close the sidebar.

Recording your progress

Tap any road to open its popup, then sign in with Google (one tap — it remembers you after that) to change its status.

If your email is on that area's Authorised list, your changes save straight away. Everyone else's changes go into a pending review queue for an authorised editor to approve — this is completely normal for most volunteers, your update just needs a quick nod from a coordinator first.

Only did part of a road?

Open the road's popup and choose Partial instead of a full status. Drag along the road to mark the exact stretch you covered — choose one side or both sides — then Save. This is the right way to record "did the north end but not the rest" instead of marking the whole road Complete.

The sync bar

The bar at the top of the sidebar shows how fresh the data is. Tap it to check for updates now; shift-click (or press-and-hold on mobile) to force a full reload if something looks stuck or wrong.

The spreadsheet

Every tracker is backed by one Google Sheet. Most people only ever need two of its tabs.

Dashboard tab

The Overall row totals every ward. Below it, a Borough Summary block gives the whole-area picture: total roads/residences, residences reached (complete-only, and a weighted figure that gives partial credit for in-progress roads), residences remaining, % reached, and how many wards are fully done versus not started at all. Four charts underneath break the same numbers down by ward — useful for deciding where to send people next.

About the residence estimate

"Residences" comes from OS Open UPRN — a national dataset of Unique Property Reference Numbers, one per addressable object. That's not just front doors: it also includes garages, sheds and other outbuildings with their own address, and individual flats within a converted house each get their own UPRN (correctly, since each one needs its own leaflet). The pipeline counts every UPRN within 40m of a road, so the figure runs a little high — a handful of non-residential addresses can slip through alongside the real ones, and the 40m buffer occasionally catches a property that actually fronts a neighbouring road.

Treat it as a ceiling rather than an exact headcount. That's the right direction to be wrong in for planning a print run, though — better to bring a few spare leaflets than run out halfway down a road.

Authorised tab

A plain list of Google account email addresses — one per row, column A — for people allowed to:

  • have their map edits save instantly instead of going to the pending queue, and
  • use the map's Admin panel (approve/deny pending changes, ban a misbehaving submitter, view or revert edit history).

To make someone an authorised editor: add their Google account email as a new row in column A. To remove access: delete their row. Either takes effect immediately — no redeploy needed, it's checked fresh every time someone signs in.

The Data tab is the master road list the map reads from — it updates itself as people report progress, so there's normally no need to hand-edit it (editing Status here skips the review/logging the map's own edit flow gives you). Changelog and Checksum are internal bookkeeping for the app — nothing to do there either.

Route Planner

A separate tool — daemeous.github.io/route-planner — for planning printed, walkable routes ahead of a leafletting event day. It's not for day-to-day tracking; that's what this map is for.

  1. Paste the link to your area's live tracker site (the one this guide is linked from) — it reads the live road data automatically.
  2. Pick a ward, a target route size (defaults to ~150 residences, roughly a 90-minute walk), and a start point — a nearby pub is suggested automatically, or set your own.
  3. Build — routes are generated in your browser in seconds.
  4. Review the routes on the map and list, then download/print route sheets (with QR codes) or publish an interactive page for your team to use on the day.

On the day, whoever's walking a route signs in with Google on the generated page — same account, same authorised-vs-pending-review rule as the main map above — and their taps write straight back to this area's own tracker Sheet. There's no separate spreadsheet to manage.

Generate routes close to the event day, not weeks ahead. Plans older than 14 days get cleaned up automatically, and a route built too early risks sending people to roads that got done in the meantime.
